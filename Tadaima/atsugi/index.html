How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

PartPointsWhat it measures
Value45Land: price per tsubo against MLIT transaction averages for that station and walk band (via Utinokati). Houses: price per m² of floor area against the median for new / used houses in this collection.
Location30Walk minutes to the nearest station, plus a station tier — Hon-Atsugi (Odakyu hub) scores highest.

Confidence10Whether the facts were verified on the listing's own detail page, and how recent its information date is. Index-page finds score lower and say so.

Freshness chips: a green New this week chip marks a property added at the latest weekly check; a Price ↓ chip marks a verified price cut since the previous check, with the amount. Both clear at the next weekly check — they describe the last seven days, not the property.

Atsugi, in numbers

Atsugi is a two-station market, and the benchmarks only ever describe those two stations. Used houses transact at about ¥88万/tsubo city-wide (floor-area basis, standardised, 1,272 deals): on the station regression curves, near-new stock runs ~118 at Hon-Atsugi and ~129 at Aiko-Ishida, falling to ~90/99 at 10 years, ~71/77 at 20 and ~61/64 at 30+. Land: Hon-Atsugi ~¥85万/tsubo (official residential points at a 7–10-minute walk print 86–103; the station's own regression of ~26 averages a 69-minute-walk catchment reaching into Aikawa and Kiyokawa and is a rural number wearing a station's name), Aiko-Ishida ~¥55万. Land falls ~25% by a 10-minute walk and ~40% by 16+ minutes; house prices show no walk gradient at all in the data. Urbanisation-control deals (~¥12万/tsubo) drag every wide average and are never comparables.

Direction: rising, moderate, commercial-led — 2026 公示地価 residential +2.4% (33 points, none down), commercial +7.3% and +9.3% in the 2026 基準地価 (the prefecture's second-fastest), with the Hon-Atsugi series now up five straight years at a plateauing pace. Standing cautions. The Sagami River's east-bank lowland (Okada, Sakai side) and the Nakatsu/Koayu corridors carry river-flood and inland-water hazard maps — the city publishes both, including house-collapse flood zones — so the lowland cards carry the check. Sediment-disaster zones are designated only on the western hill fringe (Mori-no-Sato, Iiyama, Kami-Ogino). Urbanisation-control pockets exist and are labelled where they bite. One honest negative: the Isehara Fault is real and officially evaluated, but its trace sits on the Isehara side, west of the city, at roughly 0–0.003% probability over 30 years — no fault caution for Atsugi listings is justified, and none is printed. Sources: MLIT transaction records and official land prices via Utinokati and Totinokati/tochidai/tochi-d, Ekitan for train times.
