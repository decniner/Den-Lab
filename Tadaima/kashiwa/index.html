How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

Kashiwa, in numbers

Kashiwa (population ≈438,000, Chiba's fifth-largest city and a core city) sits on the Shimosa plateau between the Tone River and Lake Teganuma. Used houses transact at about ¥100万/tsubo city-wide (floor-area basis), and age is the dominant adjuster: under-10-year stock runs ≈¥129万/tsubo, 30+-year stock ≈¥59万. Land peaks at the two planned nodes — Kashiwanoha-Campus ≈¥68万/tsubo and Kashiwa station ≈¥65万 on residential streets (its all-use average of ≈¥108万 is station-front commercial and is not used) — then steps down through Minami-Kashiwa and Shin-Kashiwa (≈¥54–56万) to the Tobu-line south-east: Masuo ¥42万, Takayanagi ¥34万, Sakasai ¥30万. The Kashiwanoha premium is real but narrow: it decays within a few streets of the station and does not leak into the older north-side districts, so every listing here is scored against its own station, never a blend.

Officially the ground keeps rising: residential land prices +5.18% in the 2026 公示地価 survey, the fifth consecutive rise, with Kashiwanoha-Campus up +7.85% — and official projections show no population cliff, with the city peaking only around 2035–2040. Three standing cautions. The east of the city (the former Shonan town, Teganuma side) is almost entirely urbanisation-control area, where building is in principle prohibited and an existing house may be non-rebuildable — its ¥6–13万/tsubo prints are that legal market, not bargains, and no listing from there appears in this collection without its status printed. Plateau-edge and yatsu-valley lots need the city's landslide-hazard check, and lowland or valley-bottom lots the prefecture liquefaction map — both are address-level checks, printed on cards where they bite. One honest negative: no nationally listed major active fault traversing Kashiwa could be verified, so this page claims none.
