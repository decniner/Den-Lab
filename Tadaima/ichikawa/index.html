How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

PartPointsWhat it measures
Value45Land: price per tsubo against MLIT transaction averages for that station and walk band (via Utinokati). Houses: price per m² of floor area against the median for new / used houses in this collection.
Location30Walk minutes to the nearest station, plus a station tier — Ichikawa and Motoyawata (JR Sobu rapid / Toei Shinjuku Line hubs) scores highest.
Space15Floor area for houses, plot size for land — with over-sized plots eased back, because very large usually means very restricted.
Confidence10Whether the facts were verified on the listing's own detail page, and how recent its information date is. Index-page finds score lower and say so.

Freshness chips: a green New this week chip marks a property added at the latest weekly check; a Price ↓ chip marks a verified price cut since the previous check, with the amount. Both clear at the next weekly check — they describe the last seven days, not the property.

Ichikawa, in numbers

Ichikawa's value is carried by its land, which is why its age gradient is shallower than Matsudo's. Used houses transact at about ¥139万/tsubo city-wide (floor-area basis, standardised): Gyotoku ~167, Myoden ~162, Motoyawata ~158 at the top and Ichikawa-Ono ~105 in the north; by age, stock under 10 years runs ~173 and 30+-year stock ~95. Land by station: Ichikawa-Mama and Sugano ~¥130万/tsubo (proxies from official points printing ~144), Ichikawa and Gyotoku ¥125万, Keisei-Yawata ~¥120万 (proxy), Myoden ¥120万, Minami-Gyotoku ¥115万, Motoyawata ¥110万 — its all-use average of ~306 is station-front commercial and is never used — Onigoe ~¥100万 (proxy), Baraki-Nakayama ¥78万, then the northern step-down: Ichikawa-Ono ¥45万 and Oomachi ¥28万. The walk gradient is steep, Tachikawa-class: land falls ~33% by a 10-minute walk and over 50% by 16+ minutes.

Direction: rising, broad, slightly decelerating — 2026 基準地価 residential +3.4% after +3.9% in 2025, with the listed station pockets up 4–6%. Standing cautions divide by landscape. South of the Edo River: river flooding plus inland water (the Ichikawa-Minami drainage district is chronic — Aug–Sep 2026 events flooded 200+ homes) and bay-side liquefaction, documented severely in the 2011 earthquake along the Urayasu–Ichikawa–Narashino strip; every Gyotoku-plain and Mama card carries the check. On the plateau edge: sediment-disaster zones (a Level-4 warning was issued for Ichikawa in September 2026), and in the north an urbanisation-control fringe whose averages are never blended into a benchmark. One researched negative: the Tokyo Bay Northern Edge Fault was formally evaluated in 2000 and judged not an active fault, so this page prints no fault caution. One house here is a stated no-rebuild (its plot does not front a Building Standards Act road) and is scored and labelled as a building-only purchase.
