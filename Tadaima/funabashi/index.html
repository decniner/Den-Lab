How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

PartPointsWhat it measures
Value45Land: price per tsubo against MLIT transaction averages for that station and walk band (via Utinokati). Houses: price per m² of floor area against the median for new / used houses in this collection.
Location30Walk minutes to the nearest station, plus a station tier — Funabashi (JR Sobu Rapid / Keisei / Tozai hub) scores highest.

Confidence10Whether the facts were verified on the listing's own detail page, and how recent its information date is. Index-page finds score lower and say so.

Freshness chips: a green New this week chip marks a property added at the latest weekly check; a Price ↓ chip marks a verified price cut since the previous check, with the amount. Both clear at the next weekly check — they describe the last seven days, not the property.

Funabashi, in numbers

Funabashi (population 652,136 — Chiba's second-largest city, still growing) is the classic Tokyo bed town written large: a planned, quieter plateau in the north and a flat, partly reclaimed bay side in the south. Used houses anchor at about ¥112万/tsubo city-wide (floor-area basis), from ≈¥150万 around Higashi-Nakayama and the port side down through the Natsumi/Narashinodai plateau at ¥130–140万. Land follows the trains: ¥105万/tsubo at Funabashi/Keisei-Funabashi, ¥115万 on the Tsudanuma side, ¥85–95万 across the Keisei Main Line and JR-side stations — then a long branch-line tail through Kita-Narashino (¥60万) and Takane-Kido (¥60万) down to Misaki and Takifudo (¥30万), which is exactly where this collection's houses cluster. Station-level pricing varies more inside Funabashi than between many neighbouring cities, so every card is scored against its own station.

Officially still rising: residential land prices +4.5% in the 2026 prefectural survey after +5.3% in 2025. Three standing cautions, all evidenced. The reclaimed bay-side south liquefied in 2011 — post-quake surveys documented house and lifeline damage across Funabashi, including in areas the hazard map rated no-risk where the old landform was low wetland — so south-side and valley-bottom listings need the old-topography check, not just the map. The plateau edge carries sediment-disaster alert zones that are not theoretical: in September 2026 the city issued a Level-4 evacuation instruction covering 9,100 households in those zones during a typhoon. And the northern and eastern fringe holds urbanisation-control listings priced at a fraction of in-zone land for legal reasons — rebuilding and financing there are restricted, and cards say so. One honest negative: no Funabashi-specific aircraft-noise evidence was found, so none is claimed.
