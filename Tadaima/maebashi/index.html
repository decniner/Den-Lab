How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

Value45Land: price per tsubo against MLIT transaction averages for that station and walk band (via Utinokati). Houses: price per m² of floor area against the median for new / used houses in this collection.

Freshness chips: a green New this week chip marks a property added at the latest weekly check; a Price ↓ chip marks a verified price cut since the previous check, with the amount. Both clear at the next weekly check — they describe the last seven days, not the property.

Maebashi, in numbers

Maebashi (population ≈330,000, Gunma's capital) is a bifurcating regional market. Used houses anchor at about ¥57万/tsubo city-wide (floor-area basis): the station, Minami-cho and Bunkyo belt ~¥70万 smoothed, Shin-Maebashi and Sosha ~¥63万, the central-east around Chuo-Maebashi, Joto and Mitsumata ~¥65万, Maebashi-Oshima and the south ~¥50万, and the Ogo/Kasukawa fringe down at ¥35–45万, with pre-1981 stock pricing at land value. Land in-zone: Chuo-Maebashi ~¥18万/tsubo on official points, Shin-Maebashi ¥17万, Maebashi and Joto ~¥16万, Mitsumata ¥15万, then a step down to Katagai ¥14万, Gunma-Soja ¥12万 and single figures on the foothills fringe. The station's commercial averages (all-use ~28, a top point at ~59) are a different asset and are never used for housing. Walk distance barely moves prices — the official-point ladder falls only ~10–15% by a 30-minute walk — so the walk bands on this page are deliberately gentle; what separates lots is zoning and district, not minutes.

Direction: official residential prices +0.1% in the 2026 公示地価 survey — flat, with centre commercial points up 2–4% and the foothill fringe down 1–1.5%. Three standing cautions. The Tone, Hirose and Momonoki river systems flood: lowland cards need the hazard-map check. The Akagi foothills carry sediment-disaster zones on their slopes (Fujimi, Miyagi, Ogo and northern Kasukawa). And the southern/western fringe plus the non-line-drawn north and east hold the classic regional trap — urbanisation-control or permit-only land where rebuilding can be barred in principle; two lots in this collection are pre-delineation residential land in the control area and are scored only against control-area comparables, with their status printed on the card. One honest negative: Mt Akagi is a JMA-listed active volcano at the lowest awareness level, but no official volcanic hazard zone covers built-up Maebashi, and none is claimed. Sources: MLIT transaction records and official land prices via Utinokati and Totinokati, Ekitan for train times.
