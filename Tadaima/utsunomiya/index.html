How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

Freshness chips: a green New this week chip marks a property added at the latest weekly check; a Price ↓ chip marks a verified price cut since the previous check, with the amount. Both clear at the next weekly check — they describe the last seven days, not the property.

Utsunomiya, in numbers

Utsunomiya (population 511,237 in the 2025 Census — Tochigi's capital and largest city) prices a full class below the Tokyo belt, and the scoring on this page refuses to pretend otherwise. Used houses anchor at about ¥69万/tsubo standardised (floor-area basis), most established districts between ¥66万 and ¥78万, with a steep age curve — a 30-year-old house prices at under half of new per tsubo. Land within walking distance of JR Utsunomiya scores at ≈¥40万/tsubo; raw catchment averages (~¥27万) describe the city's car-based fringe, not these streets. The growth story is the LRT: the corridor's official residential point has risen ~3%/yr for five straight years since the 2023 opening, the corridor gained ~5,600 residents while the city as a whole shrank, and east-side stops like Higashi-Shukugo (≈¥40万) now outprice JR suburbs like Okamoto (¥17万) and Suzumenomiya (¥18万). Every LRT figure is flagged lower-confidence — the line is three years old and its transaction series is thin.

City-wide direction is mildly rising: residential +1.2% in the 2026 公示地価， and the prefecture's all-use land prices turned positive for the first time in 34 years. Three standing cautions. The Kinugawa and Tagawa river corridors carry official flood maps warning of long-duration, wide-area inundation — the flat, cheap land east toward the Kinugawa is cheap partly for this reason, and riverside cards need the hazard-map check. The Oya district in the west sits over former stone-quarry voids that have produced surface subsidence, including a major 1989 collapse — Oya-area purchases need the quarry-void history checked like flood or zoning. And outside the rail corridors, daily life assumes a car (often two): this collection stays within walking distance on purpose.
