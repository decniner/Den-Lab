How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

PartPointsWhat it measures
Value45Land: price per tsubo against MLIT transaction averages for that station and walk band (via Utinokati). Houses: price per m² of floor area against the median for new / used houses in this collection.

Confidence10Whether the facts were verified on the listing's own detail page, and how recent its information date is. Index-page finds score lower and say so.

Freshness chips: a green New this week chip marks a property added at the latest weekly check; a Price ↓ chip marks a verified price cut since the previous check, with the amount. Both clear at the next weekly check — they describe the last seven days, not the property.

Kumagaya, in numbers

Kumagaya is a two-river, car-first city whose centre is quietly concentrating. Used houses anchor at about ¥58万/tsubo city-wide (floor-area basis; 2025 printed 60.9 on 335 deals): age is the big adjuster, from ~88 for stock under 10 years to ~27 beyond 30 years. Land in-zone: Kumagaya ~¥20万/tsubo — neither its all-use official average (22.9, folding in commercial points to ~90) nor its raw transaction catchment (14.5, dragged by a ~41-minute average walk and control-area deals at 1.6–8) — Kagohara ¥22万, Kami-Kumagaya ~¥20万 as a Kumagaya proxy, Ishihara ¥20万, and Hirose-Yachonomori ~¥13万 as a low-confidence proxy; Oaso, on the fringe, could not be isolated at all. The walk gradient is nearly flat (land −8.25% by a 10-minute walk), so the bands here are gentle and station identity matters more than minutes.

Direction: a gentle, centre-led rise — 2026 residential prices ≈+0.6% city-wide, central points up 3–5%, the fringe flat to −2%, the third straight rise after a flat 2018–2023. Standing cautions. Two rivers frame the city — the Arakawa along the southern edge and the Tone along northern Menuma — so the flood hazard-map check is lot-by-lot, not a formality. The urbanisation-control fringe is extensive (the promotion area is only ~16.5% of the city, and control land trades at ¥1.6–8万/tsubo with rebuilding restrictions); no control-area record is published in this edition, deliberately. Summer heat is real (41.1°C in July 2018 was then the national record) and, off the corridors, so is car dependence. Checked and not evidenced, so not printed: landslide zones in the built-up area and any named-fault caution. One lot in this collection is listed at ¥100万 for 165.9m² of zoned land — detail-verified as listed, implausibly cheap, and flagged as such on its card.
