How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

PartPointsWhat it measures
Value45Land: price per tsubo against MLIT transaction averages for that station and walk band (via Utinokati). Houses: price per m² of floor area against the median for new / used houses in this collection.
Location30Walk minutes to the nearest station, plus a station tier — Oyama (Tohoku Shinkansen / JR hub) scores highest.

Confidence10Whether the facts were verified on the listing's own detail page, and how recent its information date is. Index-page finds score lower and say so.

Freshness chips: a green New this week chip marks a property added at the latest weekly check; a Price ↓ chip marks a verified price cut since the previous check, with the amount. Both clear at the next weekly check — they describe the last seven days, not the property.

Oyama, in numbers

Oyama (resident-register population 166,874 in 2025, with net in-migration of +950) is a corridor market. Used houses transact at about ¥65万/tsubo city-wide (floor-area basis, recent-three-year average): the Oyama catchment ~67.7, Mamada ~56.3, with Eki-Minami (82.4) and Joto (79.3) at the top and the far-west Nakakuki at 38.9. Land in-zone: Oyama ~¥18万/tsubo — not the raw catchment's 12.8–13.3, which is dragged by the ~36% of classified deals that are urbanisation-control land at ~6.7 and by 136-tsubo average lots, and not the 43.6–45.6 station-front official points, which are commercial — and Mamada ~¥10万. Otabayashi's genuinely local level is a ~¥7万 proxy; Inuzuka/Nakakuki listings that name it score against the Oyama-west band of ~16–17, never 7. The walk gradient is the flattest yet measured (~−5.6% per 10 minutes), so the bands are gentle: the average transacted walk here is 30 minutes.

Direction: rising, corridor-led — official residential prices +1.3% in the 2026 公示地価 survey, the fastest in Tochigi and the fifth consecutive rise, with station-core points up 2–4.3% while the north-western Ryomo fringe still falls. Standing cautions: the Omoi, Tagawa, Sugata and Nagano rivers are flood-forecast rivers in Oyama and the city designated an inland-water inundation map in March 2026 — the hazard check matters on every lowland card; the fringe is urbanisation-control land whose zoning and rebuild status decides its value; and beyond the two corridors this is a car market, full stop. Honest negatives, checked: no nationally evaluated active fault runs through Oyama, there is no volcano, and sediment risk on the flat plain is low — none is claimed.
