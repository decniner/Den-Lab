How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

Value45Land: price per tsubo against MLIT transaction averages for that station and walk band (via Utinokati). Houses: price per m² of floor area against the median for new / used houses in this collection.

Freshness chips: a green New this week chip marks a property added at the latest weekly check; a Price ↓ chip marks a verified price cut since the previous check, with the amount. Both clear at the next weekly check — they describe the last seven days, not the property.

Kasukabe, in numbers

Kasukabe is the stillest market in the Tadaima set. Used houses anchor at about ¥74万/tsubo city-wide (floor-area basis, recent-three-year average; the Kasukabe station catchment standardises higher, to ~88, on a mean 17-minute walk): by age, stock under 10 years runs ~96, 20–30-year stock ~58, and 30+-year stock ~39–48. Land by station: Kasukabe ~¥48万/tsubo (residential points at an 8–13-minute walk print 47–51; the all-use average of ~50 mixes a ~122 station-front commercial point and is not the level), Yagisaki ¥36万 and Toyoharu ¥32万 from the top-up calibration, Takesato ~¥33万, Ichinowari ~¥30万 and Kita-Kasukabe ~¥26万 as flagged proxies, Fujino-Ushijima ¥25万 from its station-front official points, Himemiya ~¥22万 as a pure proxy, and Minami-Sakurai ¥19万. The walk gradient is the shallowest measured anywhere in the set — land falls only ~10–15% by an 11–15-minute walk — so the walk bands here are gentle.

Direction: flat — 2026 公示地価 residential +0.1% (median point +0.0%), 基準地価 +0.2%, with one station tax-value series unchanged for ten straight years. The standing caution here is singular and serious: flooding. Kasukabe sits in the Edo/Nakagawa lowland, and both the river-flood and inland-water hazard maps colour this city — the Metropolitan Outer Area Underground Discharge Channel mitigates the risk; it does not remove it — so both maps are the check on every card. The eastern fringe adds urbanisation-control pockets. One researched negative: the Ayasegawa fault's Kasukabe segment was judged not active (HERP, 2000), so this page prints no fault caution. Single-year district prints here rest on handfuls of deals and are ignored throughout.
