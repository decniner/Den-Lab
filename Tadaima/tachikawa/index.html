How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

Tachikawa, in numbers

Tachikawa (population ≈186,000) is the Tama region's hub — five lines through its main station, Showa Kinen Park's 165 hectares on the old airfield land, and Tokyo prices to match. Used houses transact at about ¥152万/tsubo city-wide (floor-area basis): ¥184万 in Akebono-cho, ¥178万 in Kashiwa-cho, ¥160万 in Fujimi-cho at the top, and ¥119–133万 out in the Sunagawa districts — which is why this collection lives there, and along the Seibu Haijima Line, the Tama Monorail and the JR Nambu side, rather than near the main station. Land follows the rails: about ¥150万/tsubo around Tachikawa station (a commercial-mixed average — residential streets sit below it), ¥97万 at Yagawa, ¥82万 at Nishi-Tachikawa, roughly ¥65–71万 on the Haijima-line stations, and ¥58万 at Seibu-Tachikawa, where the raw average is dragged down by very large fringe lots. The walk gradient here is the steepest of any Tadaima city so far — prices fall by roughly half between the station front and a 10-minute walk — so the Location score leans hard on minutes.

Officially the ground is rising fast: residential land prices +7.0% in the 2026 survey, the fifth consecutive rise and the fastest yet (+1.1% in 2022, accelerating every year since). Three standing cautions for buyers here. The Tachikawa fault zone （立川断層帯） is officially evaluated as M7.4-class with a 0.5–2% probability in 30 years; a University of Tokyo re-survey argues the confirmable active segment may not run under the city itself, but official maps — and insurers — still list it. The southern strip along the Tama and Zanhori rivers (Fujimi-cho above all) sits in flood-inundation zones — check the city hazard map for any address there. And streets around the disaster-prevention base and its airfield (Izumi-cho, Midori-cho) live with helicopter noise. One more thing not to oversell: the Tama Monorail's Kamikitadai–Hakone-ga-saki extension (target mid-2030s) adds no station inside Tachikawa — its value here is network, not a new stop.
