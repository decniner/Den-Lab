How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

Tokorozawa, in numbers

Tokorozawa's trump card is its junction: the Seibu Ikebukuro and Seibu Shinjuku lines cross at Tokorozawa Station, giving two independent direct routes into two Tokyo terminals — 24 minutes to Ikebukuro by express, about 46–50 minutes to Shinjuku. Land prices track that: transacted land averages ¥81万/tsubo around Tokorozawa Station, ¥64万 at Nishi-Tokorozawa and Shin-Tokorozawa, ¥54万 at Kotesashi — and falls roughly 25–40% between a 5- and a 15-minute walk in every station area.

Used houses transacted at about ¥105–112万/tsubo (floor-area basis) in 2023–26 — roughly 10% above Kawagoe — and the ground keeps rising gently: official land prices were up +2.98% city-wide in the 2026 prefectural survey, led by the station-front (+7.0% in Higashi-Jūyoshi) and Sakura Town's Higashi-Tokorozawa-Wada (+4.4%). Two cautions shape the west of the city: large areas are urbanisation-control zones where cheap land often cannot legally be rebuilt on, and the Yanase and Azuma river corridors carry mapped flood risk — both are flagged on the cards where they apply. Sources: MLIT transaction records and official land prices via Utinokati and MLIT aggregators, NAVITIME for train times.
