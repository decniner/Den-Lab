How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

PartPointsWhat it measures
Value45Land: price per tsubo against MLIT transaction averages for that station and walk band (via Utinokati). Houses: price per m² of floor area against the median for new / used houses in this collection.

Confidence10Whether the facts were verified on the listing's own detail page, and how recent its information date is. Index-page finds score lower and say so.

Freshness chips: a green New this week chip marks a property added at the latest weekly check; a Price ↓ chip marks a verified price cut since the previous check, with the amount. Both clear at the next weekly check — they describe the last seven days, not the property.

Nagareyama, in numbers

Nagareyama (2025 Census population 215,086, +7.6% — among Japan's fastest-growing cities) is a two-speed market. Used houses standardise at about ¥133万/tsubo city-wide (floor-area basis), but the city figure describes almost nowhere: the Otakanomori catchment runs ~191, Minami-Nagareyama ~150, Centralpark ~143, Hatsuishi ~124 and Edogawadai ~103; by age, stock under 10 years runs ~160 and 30+-year stock ~75. Land by station: Nagareyama-Otakanomori ~¥125万/tsubo in its core phases (its all-use average of ~169 mixes station-front commercial points to ~410 and is not used), Minami-Nagareyama ¥88万 on the city's deepest land sample, Centralpark scored at ¥70万 — not its ~58 transaction average, because every official residential point in the catchment prints 66–77 — Hatsuishi ¥62万, Heiwadai ~¥68万 and the Ryutetsu old town ~¥55万 as flagged proxies, and Edogawadai ¥54万. Inside Otakanomori the premium falls off a cliff beyond about a kilometre (official points: ~125 core, ~79 at 980 m, ~52 at 1,600 m); house prices, by contrast, show no walk penalty inside 15 minutes.

Direction: exceptionally strong and accelerating — official residential prices +13.3% in both the 2026 基準地価 and 公示地価 surveys, the fastest of any Tokyo-metro city over 100,000 people for the second straight year, with every in-brief station up 10.8–14.3%. Standing cautions. The western lowland faces Edo River flooding, and a separate inland-water map colours the Edogawadai/Hatsuishi plateau drainage — check both, by address. The north-east (Komagidai, Kamikaizuka) is urbanisation-control land whose official points rose only ~1% against the city's 13% — the two-speed city in one statistic — and its ~¥18万/tsubo transactions are never blended into a benchmark. Landslide alert zones exist in the city (a Level-4 evacuation was issued in September 2026), and one house here carries a warning-zone caution from its listing. Researched negatives: no Nagareyama fault is evidenced and no city liquefaction ranking could be verified, so neither is printed.
