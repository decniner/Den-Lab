How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

Matsudo, in numbers

Matsudo (population ≈502,000) is Chiba's third-largest city and prices like what it is: the first rank of Tokyo commuter towns, about 20 minutes from Ueno with direct trains to Tokyo and Chiyoda Line through-rides from its local stations. Used houses transact at about ¥107万/tsubo city-wide (floor-area basis) — Matsudo station catchment ≈132万, Kamihongo ≈129万, Shin-Matsudo ≈123万 at the top — and age is the sharpest adjuster of all: stock under 10 years old runs ≈139万/tsubo, 30+-year stock ≈65万. Land follows the same west–east split as everything here: about ¥70万/tsubo around Matsudo station (the headline all-use average of ≈132万 mixes station-front commercial and is not used), ¥64万 at Shin-Matsudo, ¥60万 at Kamihongo and Higashi-Matsudo — where the raw transaction regression (≈40万) is dragged down by unbuildable urbanisation-control deals and is deliberately not used — then a long tail down to Mutsumi at ¥34万. Prices fall fast with walking distance: roughly −30% by 10 minutes, −50% by 16+.

Officially the ground is rising faster here than almost anywhere in the Tokyo belt: residential land prices +8.0% in the 2026 公示地価 survey (+8.9% in 基準地価, second-fastest in the belt), the fourth straight year of acceleration. Two standing cautions. The western lowland along the Edo River and the Sakagawa sits in flood zones — check BOTH the river-flood and inland-water hazard maps before viewing there; a Shin-Matsudo condominium outside the river zone still flooded badly from inland water in a recent storm. And the eastern fringe (Kamishiki especially) holds urbanisation-control land that trades at a fraction of residential value and generally cannot be built on — any listing there is scored on land utility, never on a station benchmark. One honest negative: despite what rumours suggest, no named active fault under Matsudo is officially evidenced — the seismic caution here is the generic one (pre-1981 stock, deep-source shaking felt across north-west Chiba), and the page prints nothing stronger.
