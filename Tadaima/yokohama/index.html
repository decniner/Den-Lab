How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

PartPointsWhat it measures
Value45Land: price per tsubo against MLIT transaction averages for that station and walk band (via Utinokati). Houses: price per m² of floor area against the median for new / used houses in this collection.
Location30Walk minutes to the nearest station, plus a station tier — the edition's strongest outer-ward hubs (Totsuka, Futamatagawa, Aobadai, Nakayama) scores highest.
Space15Floor area for houses, plot size for land — with over-sized plots eased back, because very large usually means very restricted.
Confidence10Whether the facts were verified on the listing's own detail page, and how recent its information date is. Index-page finds score lower and say so.

Freshness chips: a green New this week chip marks a property added at the latest weekly check; a Price ↓ chip marks a verified price cut since the previous check, with the amount. Both clear at the next weekly check — they describe the last seven days, not the property.

Yokohama, in numbers

Yokohama (population 3,750,952 in the 2025 Census — Japan's most populous city) is eighteen wards and at least three different property markets; the only honest way to score it is locally. Used houses anchor by ward (floor-area ¥/tsubo): Totsuka ≈130, Isogo ≈132, Asahi ≈134, Konan ≈135, Seya and Izumi ≈136, Midori ≈151 — and the Aoba fringe ¥150–165 — against a city-wide figure of ≈156 that this page never uses, because it blends in centre prices no listing here competes with. Land at the edition's stations runs from Eda (≈¥117万/tsubo) and Totsuka (≈¥110万) through the Sotetsu corridor — Futamatagawa and Kibogaoka ¥82万, Tsurugamine ¥62万, Mitsukyo ¥60万 — down to Seya (¥57万) and the Izumino-line stations in the ¥60–85万 band. The one central pocket, the southern Yamate hilltop, scores at ¥116万 from the official point inside the pocket itself; the prime Yamate-cho points at ¥216–271万 describe a different, famous neighbourhood and are rejected for these streets.

Direction: outer-ward residential land rose +2 to +3.5% in the 2026 official surveys — rising, not hot, and any listing priced more than ~10% above its station anchor needs a specific reason (corner, flat, new, south road), not momentum. Standing cautions are topographic: Yokohama is a city of hills and filled valleys, so sediment-disaster (red/yellow) zones and cliff （がけ） lots are checked card by card; the Tsurumi and Kashio river lowlands carry flood depth; and the western wards hold urbanisation-control pockets whose prices are a legal market, not a discount. Several listings in this set are index-level only and say so on the card — their hazard, road and boundary status is flagged as to-verify rather than asserted.
