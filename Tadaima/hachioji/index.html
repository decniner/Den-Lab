How the ranking works

One score out of 100, built from four parts — the same maths for every property, with the reasons printed on each card. No paid placement, no hidden weighting. A major caution (for example land in an urbanisation-control area, or a house sold as a renovation project) subtracts 7 points and is always shown, never buried.

Value45Land: price per tsubo against MLIT transaction averages for that station and walk band (via Utinokati). Houses: price per m² of floor area against the median for new / used houses in this collection.

Freshness chips: a green New this week chip marks a property added at the latest weekly check; a Price ↓ chip marks a verified price cut since the previous check, with the amount. Both clear at the next weekly check — they describe the last seven days, not the property.

Hachioji, in numbers

Hachioji (population 574,428 in the 2025 Census — Tokyo's only core city and its largest municipality outside the 23 wards) is really two markets. The south is planned: Hachioji-Minamino and Minami-Osawa, new towns whose used houses transact at ¥140万/tsubo and up and whose land holds ≈¥65万/tsubo. The centre and the JR corridor price like a solid commuter hub — used houses ≈¥106万/tsubo city-wide, land ¥70万/tsubo on Hachioji's residential streets (its transaction catchment average of 34–47 is meaningless: the average deal is a 30–50-minute walk and includes urbanisation-control plots at ¥1万), ¥62万 at Keio-Hachioji, ¥60万 at Nishi-Hachioji — and then values fall away along the western corridor toward Takao (¥45万 for walkable streets) and the mountain fringe, where ¥899万 lots exist precisely because the land is hard to use.

Direction is gentle but steepening: official residential prices +4.2% in the 2026 prefectural survey after +3.9% in 2025 and ~+1%/yr before that — ignore the +16.7% figure circulating on aggregator pages, which is a point-count artefact. Population drifts slowly down while households rise, so the stock ages even as prices firm. Three standing cautions. The Asa and Yudono river lowlands (the Kitano–Naganuma belt, Katakura) sit in flood zones — check the hazard map before viewing there. The western and northern slopes carry designated landslide (土砂災害) zones, printed on cards where they bite. And the west/north fringe holds urbanisation-control land where building is banned in principle — any suspiciously cheap western lot needs its 線引き status checked before its price means anything. Sources: MLIT transaction records and official land prices via Utinokati, Ekitan for train times.
