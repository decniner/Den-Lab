# Day-trip guide usability session

**Purpose:** Observe whether a traveler can choose and use one of the five Tokyo reference outings. This is a script for future sessions; no sessions or participant results are claimed here.

## Setup

- Recruit adults who have planned or taken a Japan trip. Record relevant travel-planning experience without collecting unnecessary personal data.
- Test on a phone first; use a desktop for the same tasks if time allows.
- Use the local preview and one route page. Do not coach participants through the interface; read each task once and let them think aloud.
- Ask permission before recording. A facilitator can take notes instead.

## Tasks

1. “You are leaving from Tokyo Station in October. You have a full day and want history and food. Find an outing you would choose, then tell me why it fits and what you give up by choosing it.”
2. “Show me where you would arrive and how you would get from there to the first stop.”
3. “You now have about half a day.” Ask them to adapt the route and explain which stops they would leave out. For Sawara, observe whether they notice the limited coach schedule.
4. “The forecast has changed to steady rain.” Ask them to find a practical alternative and identify any closure or access detail they would verify.
5. “Save this plan, share it, then reopen the shared link.” Observe whether the chosen route and relevant filters return.
6. Optional: ask them to compare the selected outing with one other option and describe the deciding trade-off.

## Record

For every task, capture:

- Completion: independent / completed with a hint / not completed.
- Time to completion (seconds), from reading the task to the participant's stated answer.
- Hesitations: pauses longer than five seconds, backtracking, or repeated scanning; record where they occurred.
- Misunderstood labels, estimates, map symbols, or controls (quote the participant when possible).
- Information they expected but sought elsewhere.
- Confidence after the task (1–5), and the reason they give.
- Any accessibility barrier observed on their device or with their preferred input method.

Do not treat this small qualitative sample as representative or as evidence of field performance. Separate observed behavior from proposed fixes.
