A PLACE-LED FIELD GUIDE

Hiroshima, a city of peace

Approximate area reference · city

Reflect at the Peace Memorial Park, then spend the evening among riverside cafés and okonomiyaki counters.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

Hiroshima's Peace Memorial Park asks visitors to move from seeing to understanding. The A-Bomb Dome stands beside the river as a preserved witness to the destruction of August 1945, while the park's long axis leads toward the Cenotaph and the Peace Flame. The museum is emotionally demanding and deeply important; give it time, read the personal accounts and plan a quiet pause afterward. Across the river, central Hiroshima is a lively modern city whose everyday life makes the memorial landscape more powerful, not less. This is a place for reflection rather than quick sightseeing. Visit respectfully, keep voices low near memorials and avoid treating the site as a backdrop. Spring and autumn are comfortable for walking; hot summer days call for water and shade. A-Bomb Dome. View the preserved structure from the designated path and read the memorial context before photographing. Cenotaph and Peace Flame. Follow the park's central axis and take time with the names and inscriptions honoring victims. Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum. Allow several hours for personal histories, exhibits and reflection; check current entry hours and visitor guidance. Rest House. Pause near the park's edge for water, visitor assistance and a moment before continuing through the city.

GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

Hiroshima Station is served by the Sanyo Shinkansen and JR lines. From the station, take a Hiroshima Electric Railway tram or bus to the Peace Memorial Park area; Hiroshima Airport has limousine buses to the station. By car, use paid city garages and walk or ride transit into the park. The museum and dome are separated by a short pedestrian route.

Begin at the A-Bomb Dome, cross into the park to the Cenotaph and Peace Flame, then spend unhurried time in the museum. Allow 4-6 hours and leave a quiet interval afterward. The museum is impactful and may be distressing; visitors can pace or pause the visit.

SEASON & CONDITIONSThe park is open throughout the year and spring or autumn offers comfortable walking. August 6 is a major remembrance day with large crowds; summer heat is intense. Museum schedules and special events can change, so check the city site before visiting.

WHERE TO STAY

Stay around Hiroshima Station for rail connections or Hondori and Kamiyacho for a central base within walking or tram distance of the park.
