A PLACE-LED FIELD GUIDE

The three mountains of Dewa

Approximate area reference · attraction

Walk ancient cedar approaches into the sacred mountain landscapes of the Dewa Sanzan.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

The Dewa Sanzan pilgrimage is traditionally told as a passage through birth, death and rebirth, but a visitor need not climb every sacred peak to feel its depth. Begin at Zuishinmon Gate on Mount Haguro and enter a forest of towering cedar, where the stone stairway turns the walk into a steady act of attention. The Five-Storied Pagoda appears quietly among the trees, its wood darkened by weather and time. Continue uphill to Sanjin Gosaiden, where deities of all three mountains are enshrined beneath a massive thatched roof. Haguro is the most accessible mountain year-round; Gassan and Yudono have shorter seasonal openings and should be separate outings. A bus can reach the summit, while the full stair climb is strenuous. Dress for rain, follow shrine etiquette and never photograph prohibited sacred areas. Zuishinmon Gate. The traditional gateway begins the cedar-lined pilgrimage climb; check weather before choosing the stairs. Five-Storied Pagoda. GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

From Tokyo, travel by Shinkansen and rail to Tsuruoka. Shonai Airport has bus connections toward town. Shonai Kotsu buses serve Hagurozuishinmon and Haguro Sancho; schedules change seasonally. By car, use signed shrine parking and follow mountain road notices. Mount Gassan and Mount Yudono require separate access planning.

Take the bus to Zuishinmon, climb past the Five-Storied Pagoda and continue to Sanjin Gosaiden. Allow 4–6 hours with rests, or take the summit bus and explore on foot.
