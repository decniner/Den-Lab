More details

Start on the hill at Shuri, then descend toward Tsuboya’s pottery lanes and the city’s covered markets. Keep the last stop flexible for the day’s market hours and street activity. This route is a planning outline, not a timed tour. Check live directions and each venue’s current access information.

When to go

Naha stays warm year-round. Spring and winter are milder for walking; the rainy season is around May–June, and typhoons can affect travel from June through October. Check the forecast and official access updates. Visit Okinawa Japan · climate and seasons ↗

Route and community context: This stop order follows the existing city route record and its cited visitor sources. It is a curated route, not a representative community poll or a definitive ranking of the city’s “best” places. Some stop times or venue details may change.

Stop-by-stop guide and directions

  1. 01

    Shurijo Castle Park

    Shurijo Castle Park, Shuri, Naha, Japan

    Follow the gates and ramparts through the former Ryukyu royal precinct. Restoration and visitor routes continue to evolve; check the official park update for the areas open on your date.

    Open this stop in Google Maps ↗
  2. 02

    Tsuboya Yachimun Street

    Tsuboya Yachimun Street, Tsuboya, Naha, Japan

    Stone-paved lanes, kilns and small galleries tell the story of Okinawan pottery. Browse respectfully; many workshops are working spaces with their own opening hours.

    Open this stop in Google Maps ↗
  3. 03

    Makishi Public Market

    Makishi Public Market, Matsuo, Naha, Japan

    Explore the market floors and the surrounding arcade for Okinawan ingredients and everyday food culture. Check current floor access and vendor hours before planning a meal here.

    Open this stop in Google Maps ↗
  4. 04

    Kokusai-dori & Heiwa-dori

    Kokusai-dori & Heiwa-dori, Naha city centre, Japan

    Finish among the central shopping streets and covered lanes. The neighbourhood is liveliest later in the day; Sundays may bring pedestrian-only activity on parts of the main street.
