A MERCHANT TOWN ONCE LINKED TO EDO

Kawagoe,
the Little Edo.

A day between Kawagoe Station and the old town

Hear the bell above the tiled roofs. Step inside the last rooms of a castle palace. Then follow the old merchant street by scent, one sweet potato confection at a time.

Follow the mapped day ↓Plan your arrival

“Little Edo” is more than an old-street backdrop. Kawagoe was a castle town and a trading partner to Edo; its goods travelled by river, and its merchants built storehouses designed to protect what they sold. The best day here moves through those layers: a quiet palace built in 1848, a street of fire-resistant kura, a bell that still marks the hours, and a lane where the town’s sweet-potato story ends in something you can taste.

FOUR DETAILS THAT MAKE THE DAY

Read the town at street level

01 · THE CASTLE

Honmaru Goten

The surviving palace was built in 1848. Its restrained rooms, painted sliding doors and broad hall offer an unusually intimate look at a castle household; most of Kawagoe Castle is gone, so this fragment matters.

02 · THE TRADE STREET

Kurazukuri Ichibangai

Look above today’s shop signs. Thick walls, heavy shutters and dark rooflines tell you these were working merchant storehouses, built for a town where fire could erase a fortune.

03 · THE HOUR

Toki no Kane

The bell tower remains part of an ordinary townscape, not a museum display. Its bell rings at 6:00, noon, 3:00 and 6:00; if your timing works, pause in the lane and listen.

04 · THE TASTE

Kashiya Yokocho

About 30 confectionery shops line this compact alley. Kawagoe’s sweet-potato identity grew from produce shipped by boat toward Edo; here the history becomes a warm snack, a sweet or a bag for the train home.

The first three stops show the town’s structure; the last gives the visit its flavour. Shop selection and opening times vary by day.

THE DAY, IN ORDER · BUS ACCESS + OLD-TOWN WALK

From castle rooms to candy lane

Begin at Kawagoe Station and take a local bus toward Honmaru Goten, or make the longer walk if you want to see more of the city between the station and the castle district. From the palace, continue toward the Kurazukuri streets, pause at Toki no Kane, and finish in Kashiya Yokocho. Allow time to browse: this is a day made of close details, small shops and unhurried side streets.

GETTING THERE

Easy rail access; choose your station

From Ikebukuro: Tobu Tojo Line to Kawagoe Station; the Kawagoe Limited Express can take as little as 26 minutes.

From Shinjuku: JR Saikyo Line rapid service to Kawagoe Station takes about 50 minutes, or Seibu Shinjuku Line limited express “Koedo” to Hon-Kawagoe in as little as 44 minutes. These are different stations; check which one best fits your onward route.

From Omiya: JR Kawagoe Line to Kawagoe Station, about 20 minutes. From Shibuya, the Fukutoshin Line route takes about 45 minutes. Times and service patterns vary; check the operator for your travel date.

From Narita or Haneda: Travel by rail into Tokyo and connect toward Ikebukuro, Shinjuku or Omiya. Compare live transfers before choosing your Kawagoe station; airport-to-town trips involve multiple services.

By car: The tourism association lists Kawagoe IC on the Kan-Etsu Expressway for Tokyo-side access. Parking and traffic can add time around the historic center; use official parking updates and consider rail on busy weekends.

FIELD NOTES

A few things to check before you go

  • Honmaru Goten closes on set days. Check its official calendar and admission details before making it your first stop.
  • Arrive with room to wander. The historic streets are active shopping streets; shop hours differ, and weekends can be busy.
  • Take the bell as a bonus, not a schedule anchor. It rings four times daily—6:00, 12:00, 15:00 and 18:00—but do not rush the rest of the route to catch it.
  • Try the local sweet-potato tradition. Choose a snack that travels well if you plan to continue exploring.
