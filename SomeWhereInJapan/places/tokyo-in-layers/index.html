A PLACE-LED FIELD GUIDE

Tokyo, in layers

Approximate area reference · city

A city of side streets and skylines, quiet shrines and one more tiny bar.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

Tokyo, in layers rewards travelers who treat the capital as a series of neighborhoods instead of a checklist. Begin in Asakusa, where the Kaminarimon lantern, Nakamise's shopfronts and the temple precinct create a walkable introduction to older Tokyo. Ride west and the city changes scale: Harajuku's busy streets meet the wooded approach to Meiji Jingu, then the broad crossing at Shibuya gathers the movement of the modern city into one memorable pause. Tokyo Station and Marunouchi offer another contrast, with the red-brick station facade opening toward wide avenues and the Imperial Palace grounds. Each district works as its own half-day; combining two nearby areas makes a satisfying day, while trying to cross the city repeatedly turns the journey into station transfers. Spring brings blossoms and comfortable outdoor walks; June is humid and rainy, so keep an indoor museum or aquarium in reserve. For two connected districts, plan about 6 to 8 hours, then add time for exhibitions, observation decks or meals. Admission varies by attraction, and most landmarks have separate opening schedules.

GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

Air: Haneda and Narita both connect to central Tokyo by train, airport bus and taxi; Haneda is closer to the city. Train: Tokyo is served by the shinkansen from other major Japanese cities and by JR, Tokyo Metro, Toei Subway and private railways within the capital. Bus: highway coaches serve Shinjuku and Tokyo Station, with airport buses to major districts. Car: use public transport within the central city when possible; parking and traffic vary by district. For Asakusa, Tokyo Metro Ginza Line, Toei Asakusa Line and Tobu Skytree Line serve Asakusa Station; Shibuya and Shinjuku have many rail operators.

For a first full day, pair Asakusa with a nearby eastern-Tokyo stop. On another day, combine Meiji Jingu and Harajuku with Shibuya. Keep the districts separate unless there is a clear reason to cross town; this is a planning pattern rather than a timed route. Allow 6 to 8 hours for two connected districts and check admission or reservation details for museums and observatories.
