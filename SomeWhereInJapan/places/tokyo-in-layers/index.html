More details

Start at Senso-ji, then follow the numbered stops to Skytree, Ueno and Akihabara. This is a full day; use the individual map links to skip a stop if you need more rest.

Best seasonal fit: late March to early April for Ueno’s cherry blossom period; October and November for cooler park walks. Blossom timing shifts each year.

How to follow the route

  1. Start at Senso-ji. Follow the temple approach from Kaminarimon through Nakamise Street. Stay on the main paths and take your time.
  2. Go to Tokyo Skytree. Walk or use live directions. Choose the paid observation decks or enjoy the tower and shops from ground level.
  3. Continue to Ueno. Use the live directions for the rail trip. Choose one museum or enjoy Ueno Park and Ameyoko.
  4. Finish in Akihabara. Follow the directions from Ueno and explore the electric-town streets. Shops have different hours.

Opening and access notes

Senso-ji’s main hall is listed as open 06:00–17:00 from April through September and 06:30–17:00 from October through March. Other halls may follow different hours. The official Senso-ji access page lists Asakusa stations on the Tobu, Tokyo Metro Ginza, Tsukuba Express and Toei Asakusa lines; it also notes that the temple has no visitor parking.

Ueno Park itself is open at all times and free to enter; some facilities inside the park charge admission or keep separate hours. Individual Ameyoko shops set their own opening times; see the GO TOKYO Ueno guide for area context.

Useful choices

  • Tokyo National Museum for Japanese and Asian art and archaeology; confirm the day’s galleries and ticket details before setting out.
  • National Museum of Nature and Science for natural history and science.
  • National Museum of Western Art for Western art and architecture.
  • Prefer open-air time? Give more of the visit to Ueno Park and the pond, then browse Ameyoko without adding another museum.

More details

Begin at Tsukiji Outer Market in the morning, then continue to Tokyo Station and the Imperial Palace East Gardens.

Best seasonal fit: spring and autumn for the garden paths. In winter, visit early because the gardens close earlier and have weekday closures.

How to follow the route

  1. Start at Tsukiji Outer Market. Go in the morning for the liveliest food and shopping. Each shop sets its own hours; many close before evening.
  2. Continue to Tokyo Station. Use live transit directions, then view the Marunouchi red-brick façade and plaza.
  3. Finish at the East Gardens. Walk toward an open garden gate. Check the official opening calendar before you go.

East Gardens: current visitor information

Admission is free. The gardens are normally closed on Mondays and Fridays and from December 28–January 3, or when official functions require closure. They open on national holidays except the Emperor’s Birthday (February 23); when a national holiday falls on Monday, the following Tuesday is closed. Check the official calendar for exceptions. Baggage inspection takes place at the entrance, so travel light or leave large luggage at your hotel or a station locker.

Start at Shinjuku Gyoen, then head south to Meiji Jingu, Harajuku and Shibuya. Check the garden and shrine hours before you set out.

Best seasonal fit: spring and autumn for a comfortable forest walk. In summer, start early and save the Shibuya stop for later; Meiji Jingu’s hours follow sunrise and sunset.

How to follow the route

  1. Start at Shinjuku Gyoen. Walk the garden paths and check the official gate hours, closure days and admission.
  2. Go to Meiji Jingu. Use live transit directions from Shinjuku Gyoen. The shrine approach is a long, shaded walk through the forest.
  3. Continue through Harajuku. Walk toward Harajuku Station and nearby shopping streets. Shops and cafés set their own hours.
  4. Finish at Shibuya Crossing. Follow the streets south to the crossing. Use live directions if you prefer the train.

Meiji Jingu: hours and access

The shrine grounds are free and open daily from sunrise to sunset; the gate times change by month. See official hours. From each entrance, allow at least another 10 minutes on foot to reach the main shrine building. Wheelchairs are available at the grounds entrance; see official access information. The Inner Garden and museum have separate hours and admission.

Respectful shrine visit

Keep to the paths, avoid photography or video at the main sanctuary, and eat, drink or smoke only in designated areas. If you wish, follow the official etiquette guide for shrine visits; visitors are also welcome simply to observe quietly.

Plan one Tokyo day at a time

These are three separate neighborhood clusters. If you have one day, pick the route that best matches your interests. With two days, choose two clusters; with three, give each one its own day. Trying to cover East, Central and West Tokyo in one day adds cross-city transfers and leaves less time at the places themselves.

The map pins show ordered stops. Dashed connectors are schematic and do not promise a walking path, station transfer or travel time. Use the Google Maps route button for live directions from your actual starting point.

Why these places are on the routes

Traveler itineraries often mention Tokyo Skytree, Akihabara, Tsukiji and Shinjuku alongside the original stops. These are recurring suggestions, not a formal vote or a universal “best” list. I added them to nearby routes so the plan stays grouped by area.

Community signals are self-selected. Tripadvisor’s list uses reviews, ratings, page views and user location; itinerary forums are individual trip reports. Getting around Tokyo

  • A rechargeable IC card is the simplest option for most train and subway journeys. Tap in and out and check the operator for unusual routes or limited-express supplements.
  • The Tokyo Subway Ticket covers Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway lines for 24, 48 or 72 hours. As checked 24 October 2026, adult prices are ¥1,000, ¥1,500 and ¥2,000. It does not cover JR or private railways. The product is for overseas visitors and domestic visitors living outside Tokyo, Kanagawa, Saitama, Chiba, Ibaraki, Tochigi, Gunma and Yamanashi; verify eligibility and current prices before buying.
  • Several operators serve each major station. Check the line, station name and exit in a live route planner; “Tokyo Station,” “Shibuya” and “Ueno” each have multiple gates and connecting corridors.
  • At busy stations, follow platform and escalator markings, stand clear of train doors, and keep luggage compact. Leave extra margin when changing operators or navigating with a stroller or wheelchair.

Arriving by air

Haneda and Narita both connect to central Tokyo, but the best rail or bus service depends on your hotel and first route. Compare the official airport access options before choosing a ticket; include the airport transfer when deciding whether a subway pass is worthwhile.

Weather and best walking conditions

  • Spring: Comfortable for outdoor walks on many days, but mornings and evenings can still be cool; bring layers.
  • Rainy season: June to mid-July is often wet and humid. Carry a breathable rain layer and keep a museum or indoor stop as a backup.
  • Summer: Heat and humidity can make long outdoor stretches tiring. Take shade and water breaks; avoid trying to rush all three routes.
  • Autumn and winter: Often good for walking, but daylight is shorter in winter and shrine or garden closing hours may limit late starts.

Accessibility, luggage and comfort

Meiji Jingu reports wheelchairs available at its grounds entrance and notes that the walk from each entrance to the main shrine takes at least another 10 minutes. This does not certify every path or transfer in the West Tokyo route as step-free. Check station elevator and barrier-free details with the operator before travel. The Imperial Palace East Gardens inspect bags at entry; travel with a small day bag or use a station or hotel locker.

Wear comfortable shoes for station corridors and park or shrine paths. In rain, some outdoor surfaces can be slippery. If you need a quiet break, choose a park bench, café or indoor museum rather than pressing on to the next district.
