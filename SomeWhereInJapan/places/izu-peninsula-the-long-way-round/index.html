A starting point

What makes this place worth exploring

Izu makes more sense as a set of bases than as one hurried loop. The official national tourism guide separates eastern resort towns such as Atami and Ito from Shimoda in the south, the rugged capes, and the peninsula’s inland highlands; it advises allowing several days to explore.

Seasonal guidance has not been independently verified yet.

Pick one base—such as Atami, Ito or Shimoda—and build a focused day around that area. The JNTO guide identifies the Shinkansen route from Tokyo to Atami, but this does not make the far-south or west-coast sights a same-day extension.

Before you go

Getting there

The JNTO guide lists Tokyo Station to Atami by Tokaido Shinkansen at about 50 minutes, with the Odoriko limited express and slower Tokaido Line as alternatives. Confirm the current timetable and continue from Atami according to the selected Izu area.

  • Rail: The official guide describes Tokyo–Atami by Tokaido Shinkansen and Odoriko limited express; check onward local connections for the selected base. (Izu Peninsula destination guide)
