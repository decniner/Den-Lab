A PLACE-LED FIELD GUIDE

Matsumoto beneath the Alps

Approximate area reference · attraction

A striking black castle anchors a walkable city at the foot of Japan’s Northern Alps.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

Matsumoto Castle’s black keep seems to float above its moat, with the Northern Alps often visible behind it on clear days. It is a National Treasure and one of Japan’s oldest surviving keeps; steep stairs and low ceilings make the defensive design unmistakable. After the castle, town lanes reveal an everyday side. Nawate Street follows the Metoba River and is known for frog motifs, shops and snack stops. Nearby Nakamachi’s storehouse facades recall the merchant town beyond the moat. These sites are close enough to walk, so the visit can unfold without constant transit. Former Kaichi School adds an example of early modern education if time permits. Summer festivals bring visitors, while winter is crisp and quieter. Start early at the castle to avoid queues, then slow down over lunch in the old town. Matsumoto Castle. Walk the moat and enter the National Treasure keep; steep internal stairs require care. Nawate Street and Yohashira Shrine. Browse the riverfront lane and its frog motifs, then pause at the nearby shrine. Nakamachi Street. See storehouse facades and browse craft shops, cafes and local food.

GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

JR trains connect Matsumoto with Nagano and Shinjuku; highway buses reach Tokyo, Nagoya and Takayama. Matsumoto Airport has bus and taxi links. The castle is about a 15-minute walk from the station; Nawate and Nakamachi are nearby. Use public parking outside the moat.

Start at Matsumoto Castle, continue on foot to Nawate Street and Yohashira Shrine, then cross to Nakamachi. Add Former Kaichi School if time allows. Allow 4–6 hours.
