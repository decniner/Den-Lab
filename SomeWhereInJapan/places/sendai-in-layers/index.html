When to go

Stop-by-stop guide and directions

  1. 01

    Sendai Asaichi (Morning Market)

    Sendai Asaichi, Chuo, Aoba Ward, Sendai, Japan

    This compact market is known as the city kitchen: look for Sanriku seafood, seasonal Miyagi produce, breakfast bowls, and freshly fried croquettes. The official visitor guide places it five minutes from Sendai Station’s west exit. Go in the morning, check the day before travelling, and expect individual stalls to set their own hours; the guide lists Sundays and national holidays as closure days.

  2. 02

    Zuihoden Mausoleum

    23-2 Otamayashita, Aoba Ward, Sendai, Miyagi 980-0814, Japan

    Date Masamune, founder of the Sendai domain, chose this hillside as his final resting place. The richly colored mausoleum sits among cedar trees, so the visit shifts from ornate Momoyama-style detail to a quiet, shaded approach. Allow 45 to 60 minutes. The official access notes about 80 steep steps to reception and around 30 more across the grounds; visitors who need step-free access should check with the venue before making this a fixed stop. Loople’s stop is Zuihoden-mae, followed by a walk of about seven minutes.

    Open this stop in Google Maps ↗
  3. 03

    Sendai City Museum

    26 Kawauchi, Aoba Ward, Sendai, Miyagi 980-0862, Japan

    The museum stands on the former Sannomaru grounds of Sendai Castle and holds roughly 100,000 objects connected with the Date family and the region. Look for the story behind Date Masamune’s black-lacquered armor and the Keicho-era mission to Europe. The permanent display changes seasonally, and the armor is shown only during selected periods, so check the current exhibition before choosing this as the day’s anchor. Allow about one to one and a half hours. The Loople stop is shared with Sendai International Center.

    Open this stop in Google Maps ↗
  4. 04

    Sendai Castle Site and Kenbunkan

    Sendai Castle Site, Aoba-yama, Aoba Ward, Sendai, Japan

    The Honmaru occupied a hill about 130 metres above the city. From the open castle site, the view reaches across Sendai and, on clear days, toward the Pacific; the equestrian statue of Date Masamune is the landmark most visitors look for. Before walking the grounds, step into Kenbunkan, the free historical information facility, for models and displays that make the stone walls and layout easier to read. Allow about 45 to 60 minutes for the museum and castle-site walk together. Loople stops at Sendai Castle Site.

    Open this stop in Google Maps ↗
  5. 05

    Jozenji-dori Avenue

    Jozenji-dori Avenue, Kokubuncho, Aoba Ward, Sendai, Japan

    End beneath the zelkova canopy on the central promenade. The avenue takes its name from a temple that no longer stands, linking the modern city back to Sendai’s founding era. In spring and summer the branches make a green tunnel; autumn turns the leaves gold, and in winter the Pageant of Starlight lights the trees. Sculptures and shops give the walk a distinctly urban feel. Loople serves the Jozenji-dori Avenue / City Hall stop. The avenue is public space, so time needed depends on how long you choose to wander.
