A PLACE-LED FIELD GUIDE

Iwami Ginzan’s forest paths

Approximate area reference · attraction

Quiet trails connect a former silver-mining settlement with wooded hills and preserved streets.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

Iwami Ginzan is a cultural landscape where a former silver mine, a preserved town and mountain forest belong to one story. Begin at the World Heritage Center to understand the mine's scale and plan transport: private vehicles do not serve the historic core, and buses or walking connect the dispersed sites. Omori's narrow street follows the river past merchant houses, temples and small workshops. Beyond town, the path toward Ryugenji Mabu enters a quiet valley where the mine tunnel makes the labor and engineering tangible. This is a place to notice how nature reclaimed the industrial landscape, not just to collect a heritage-site stamp. The walking distances are substantial, buses limited and some terrain uneven. Visit in spring or autumn, start early, and check mine access and shuttle availability before setting out. World Heritage Center. GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

Travel by JR Sanin Main Line to Odashi Station, then take the local bus to Iwami Ginzan World Heritage Center, roughly 25-35 minutes depending on service. Izumo Airport is the nearest major regional airport; buses and trains connect onward. Drivers should park at the Heritage Center and use public buses or walk into Omori. Services are infrequent, so plan the return carefully.

Orient at the World Heritage Center, take the bus to Omori, walk the historic town and continue toward Ryugenji Mabu only if time and mobility allow. Allow 5-7 hours. The mine trail adds a significant round trip; confirm shuttle operation and last bus before starting.
