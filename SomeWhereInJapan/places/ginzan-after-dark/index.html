A PLACE-LED FIELD GUIDE

Ginzan after dark

Approximate area reference · attraction

A lantern-lit hot spring town where winter feels like stepping into another time.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

Ginzan Onsen is a short, narrow riverside town whose atmosphere depends on staying long enough to notice small details. Wooden inns line the Ginzan River, and after dusk the lamps catch their façades, bridges and the moving water. Look for kote-e relief panels on the buildings, then follow the lane upstream to Shirogane Waterfall; on the way back, Warashiyu footbath offers a brief pause beside the river. The route is compact, but the streets are narrow and shared with residents, inn guests and service vehicles, so keep to the pedestrian areas and follow local entry rules. Winter snow gives the town its famous contrast, but it also brings visitor controls, road restrictions and heavy demand for buses and lodging. Access is via Oishida Station on the Yamagata Shinkansen and a bus of about 40 minutes; confirm the current reservation or shuttle requirements before traveling, especially in winter. The onsen town is a better overnight destination than a hurried late-night detour. Allow two to three hours in the town itself, plus the transfer time, and book accommodation well ahead for peak dates. Shiroganebashi Bridge, Warashiyu footbath and Shirogane Waterfall shape the riverside walk.

GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

Take the Yamagata Shinkansen to Oishida Station, then the scheduled bus to Ginzan Onsen, about 40 minutes. Yamagata Airport is about an hour by car. By car, use the designated parking and shuttle system; do not follow narrow mountain roads that the official access guide warns against. Seasonal visitor controls and reservations may apply, especially in winter. Check the official notice before travel.

Walk from Shiroganebashi through the inn-lined lane to Warashiyu footbath and Shirogane Waterfall, then return through the same compact town. Allow about 3 hours in Ginzan, plus transfers; an overnight stay is recommended.

WHERE TO STAY

Book a Ginzan ryokan well ahead for an overnight visit; Obanazawa is the alternative base when rooms are unavailable.

FEES & VISIT TIMETown walks and the public footbath are generally free with no admission charge; lodging, bathing and guided tours have separate charges. Check visitor-control notices.
