A PLACE-LED FIELD GUIDE

The blue of Biei

Approximate area reference · attraction

A still, almost impossible shade of blue tucked into Hokkaido’s green interior.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

The Blue Pond is not a natural lake discovered unchanged in the forest. It formed during a 1980s erosion-control project, when a dam was built to protect Biei from volcanic mudflows. Mineral-rich water from the Shirahige Falls area enters the Biei River, and fine particles scatter light so the pond shifts between milky turquoise and deep cobalt. The pale larch trunks are dead trees, their stillness part of the scene rather than a planted display. Start at the designated paths and viewing area, then continue to Blue River Bridge for a different look at the blue water and falls; these are separate stops, so allow for the drive or bus connection. Biei's famous patchwork fields lie farther away and are working farmland: view them from public roads and never enter planted fields. The colour changes with weather, light and season, and winter access can be affected by snow or severe conditions. Plan two to three hours for the pond and falls, longer for the broader Biei countryside, and check local road and bus information before you go. Shirogane Blue Pond, Shirahige Falls and Blue River Bridge make three distinct views of the same water system.

GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

The Blue Pond is about 20 minutes by car from JR Biei Station and about 40 minutes from Asahikawa Airport. By rail, reach Biei on the JR Furano Line, then use a seasonal bus, taxi or rental car; direct public transport is limited. Flights arrive through Asahikawa Airport, then continue by road. Parking is available and paid. By car, combine the pond with Shirogane Onsen and Shirahige Falls, but verify road conditions in winter.

Walk the designated pond paths, then transfer to Blue River Bridge and Shirahige Falls. Allow about 3 hours for these stops; add half a day for the broader Biei countryside.
