Kiso Valley post towns

Approximate area reference · walking-route

Begin in Magome, walk the old Nakasendo over the pass to Tsumago, and use local bus and rail for the return. Plan a considered route, transport and memorable details with this Nagano · Chubu field guide.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

The Nakasendo route through Kiso Valley once carried travelers between Edo and Kyoto, and its post towns make that journey legible in the landscape. Magome rises along a stone-paved slope, with water channels, inns and timber storefronts arranged for people arriving on foot. Beyond town, the trail crosses forest toward Tsumago; teahouses and a bear-warning bell remind walkers this is a rural path, not a themed street. Tsumago’s preservation rules helped keep modern signs and overhead wires away from its central lanes, and evening quiet feels different from a day-trip crowd. The classic Magome–Tsumago walk is roughly eight kilometres and usually takes three to four hours, with direction and elevation affecting effort. Buses link both ends, but schedules are limited and seasonal. Carry water, check the return timetable, and leave time to explore both towns rather than treating them only as trailheads. Magome-juku. Walk the sloped post town early, when water channels and timber storefronts are quiet. Nakasendo forest trail. Follow the marked historic route through woods and hamlets; heed local trail notices. Tsumago-juku. Explore the preserved post town after the walk, including former honjin and wakihonjin buildings.

GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

Reach Magome from Nakatsugawa Station by bus or car; Tsumago is served from Nagiso Station by bus or taxi. JR Chuo Line connects with Nagoya and Matsumoto. The one-way trail needs a planned bus or taxi return. Use parking outside preserved streets.

Walk from Magome through the forest trail to Tsumago, pausing at rest areas, then use bus or pre-arranged taxi to return. The walk is about 8 km and 3–4 hours; allow 5–7 hours with both towns.

WHERE TO STAY

Stay in a minshuku in Tsumago or Magome for quiet evenings; Nakatsugawa and Nagiso offer rail-linked lodging.
