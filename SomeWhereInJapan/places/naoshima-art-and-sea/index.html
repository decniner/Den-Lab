A PLACE-LED FIELD GUIDE

Naoshima, art and sea

Approximate area reference · attraction

Island light, contemporary art, and a slower rhythm between ferries.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

Naoshima's art is inseparable from its island setting. A bright pumpkin beside the port can feel playful, while Chichu Art Museum turns natural light into part of the work itself. The island's quiet roads, fishing hamlets and sea views connect installations and museums, but distances are longer than they look on a map. Reserve timed museum entry well ahead, especially for Chichu, and plan around ferry and local bus schedules. Benesse House blends art, architecture and accommodation; visitors should follow its photography and access rules. The best day leaves space between venues rather than treating the island as a checklist. Spring and autumn bring comfortable cycling weather; summer heat and ferry crowds require planning. Respect residents and do not photograph private homes. End at the port as the light fades over the Inland Sea. Miyanoura Port. GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

Ferries to Naoshima's Miyanoura Port run from Uno Port in Okayama and Takamatsu Port in Kagawa. Uno is reached by JR from Okayama; Takamatsu has rail, airport bus and domestic flights. Cars can use some island roads, but parking is limited and bicycles or local buses suit most visitors. Museum access may require advance reservation; check port-specific ferry schedules.

Arrive at Miyanoura, see the port-side art, then take the island bus or bicycle to Chichu and the Benesse area. Allow 6-8 hours and reserve museum slots before buying transport. Leave time for ferry queues and the return crossing.
