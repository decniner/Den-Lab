A PLACE-LED FIELD GUIDE

The Iya Valley

Approximate area reference · region

Deep green ravines and vine bridges in one of Japan’s quieter corners.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

The Iya Valley folds deep into Shikoku's mountains, where roads cling to cliffs above the emerald Iya River and hamlets appear on steep slopes. The vine bridge at Kazurabashi is the best-known crossing, but its appeal lies in its setting: woven vines, rushing water and forested walls compress the senses into a brief, exhilarating passage. Nearby, Biwa Falls and the small settlement are best explored slowly, while the Peeing Boy statue offers a dramatic roadside viewpoint farther along the gorge. These places are not a continuous footpath; narrow roads and sparse buses make transport choices central to the day. A rental car gives flexibility, but mountain driving takes concentration. Spring green and autumn colour are lovely; rain can make paths slick. Check bus returns and bridge operating conditions before descending into the valley. JR Oboke Station. Check the bus timetable before leaving the station; services to remote valley sites are limited. Iya Kazurabashi. Cross only if open and comfortable with heights; the vine bridge is narrow and can sway above the river. Biwa Falls. Walk from the bridge area to the nearby waterfall and shrine setting, watching wet or uneven paths. Peeing Boy viewpoint. Reach this cliffside statue by road rather than assuming a connecting walking trail; use a safe marked pull-off.

GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

From JR Oboke Station, Shikoku Kotsu buses reach Kazurabashi in about 20 minutes when services operate. Takamatsu Airport is the most useful air gateway, with onward bus and rail connections; Tokushima Airport is another option. Drivers use National Route 32 and mountain roads, with limited parking at viewpoints. Bus service is sparse, so confirm the timetable and do not assume attractions are walk-connected.

Use Oboke Station as the transport hub, visit Kazurabashi and Biwa Falls, then add the Peeing Boy viewpoint only with a car or suitable bus connection. Allow 5-7 hours including mountain transfers. Roads are narrow; keep daylight and weather in mind.

WHERE TO STAY

Stay around Oboke for rail and river access, or in a valley onsen inn near Kazurabashi to avoid rushing the last bus back.
