A PLACE-LED FIELD GUIDE

Arashiyama’s green corridor

Approximate area reference · attraction

Bamboo rises above the path in a luminous grove on Kyoto’s western edge.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

Arashiyama is often reduced to one bamboo photograph, but its best day is a sequence of river, garden and neighborhood. Enter the Bamboo Grove early, before the narrow lane fills, and keep moving rather than stopping in the flow. Tenryuji offers a quieter counterpoint: its garden uses surrounding hills as borrowed scenery, so the landscape seems to continue beyond the temple wall. Drift toward Togetsukyo Bridge, whose name evokes a moon crossing the river; the view changes with rain, season and water level. Cross to the south-bank lanes and hillside viewpoints if time allows—the area is larger than the photo corridor. Sagano’s quieter streets and museums reward an afternoon, and the Randen tram or JR Sagano Line makes arrival straightforward. The bamboo path is public and free, while gardens charge admission. Never carve or tie objects to bamboo. Visit early, then let the river set the pace. Arashiyama Bamboo Grove. Follow the public lane early and keep moving so everyone can enjoy the narrow path. Tenryuji Temple garden. See the pond garden framed by hills; allow time to walk the full grounds. Togetsukyo Bridge and riverbank. Take in changing river and mountain views, then explore the south-bank lanes if time allows.

GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

From Kyoto Station take the JR Sagano Line to Saga-Arashiyama; Randen serves the area from central Kyoto and Hankyu reaches the south bank. Kansai Airport connects via JR Haruka and rail transfers. By car, use paid lots outside the busy center. The grove, Tenryuji and bridge are walkable.

Walk the Bamboo Grove at first light, enter Tenryuji’s garden when it opens, then follow the river to Togetsukyo Bridge. Allow 4–6 hours; continue to Saga or the south bank if time permits.

RIVER, GROVE, MOUNTAIN

Give Arashiyama more than one photograph

The bamboo path is only the opening scene. The district becomes more rewarding when the river, temple gardens and mountain backdrop have time to come into view.

Move with the landscape

Arrive early for the Bamboo Grove, then continue toward Tenryuji’s garden if it is open and suits your plans. From there, let the day widen toward the Katsura River: bridges, boat traffic and the wooded slopes give Arashiyama a calmer scale than the busiest lanes near the station.

Do not force a long cross-district itinerary into the morning. Some nearby sights involve extra walking or a separate transport leg; check the map and current opening information before adding them.

BEST FOR

Garden lovers, gentle walks, river scenery and travelers who want a nature-led contrast to central Kyoto.

PACE & COMFORT

Allow half a day for the central district, longer if you add temple interiors or a boat experience. The grove is busiest from midmorning onward.

MAKE IT YOURS

Visit the grove early, then slow down by the water. In peak foliage and blossom seasons, expect crowded paths and plan extra time.
