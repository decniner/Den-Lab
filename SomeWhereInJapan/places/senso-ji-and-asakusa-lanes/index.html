Explore the route

Asakusa’s appeal comes from how its temple approach, neighborhood streets and Sumida waterfront sit within an easy walking area. GO TOKYO links Sensoji, Kaminarimon and Nakamise with nearby river views; the district lets a visitor move from a busy gate to the open river edge without changing neighborhoods.

THE ROUTE · 4 STOPS

Tokyo: station streets, old Asakusa & Ueno

Begin at Tokyo Station and the Imperial Palace edge, then cross to Asakusa for the old-city streets and finish among Ueno’s museums, park paths and market lanes. This city-centre sequence follows GO TOKYO’s first-visit route logic; check opening days and current access before setting out.

  1. 01

    Marunouchi, Chiyoda, Tokyo

    Tokyo Station & Marunouchi

    Tokyo Station & Marunouchi, Marunouchi, Chiyoda, Tokyo, Japan

    Start with the red-brick station façade, then step into Marunouchi’s broad avenues. It is a useful orientation point: old rail architecture, modern Tokyo and the palace district meet within a short walk.

  2. 02

    Chiyoda, Tokyo

    Imperial Palace East Gardens

    Imperial Palace East Gardens, Chiyoda, Tokyo, Japan

    The former Edo Castle grounds make the city’s scale suddenly quiet. The East Gardens have scheduled closure days and occasional access changes, so check the Imperial Household Agency’s visitor notice before planning this stop.

    Open this stop in OpenStreetMap ↗
  3. 03

    Asakusa, Taito, Tokyo

    Asakusa & Senso-ji

    Asakusa & Senso-ji, Asakusa, Taito, Tokyo, Japan

    Walk from Kaminarimon through Nakamise toward Senso-ji, then give yourself time to turn into the smaller lanes beyond the main approach. The side streets reveal a neighbourhood as well as a landmark.

    Open this stop in OpenStreetMap ↗
  4. 04

    Ueno, Taito, Tokyo

    Ueno Park & Ameyoko

    Ueno Park & Ameyoko, Ueno, Taito, Tokyo, Japan

    Choose a museum or a slow loop through the park before finishing around Ameyoko’s lively market lanes. Museum schedules differ, and many close on a weekly day; check the specific venue if you want to go inside.

GETTING THERE

Choose how to arrive

Asakusa Station is served by the Tokyo Metro Ginza Line, Toei Asakusa Line and Tobu Skytree Line. GO TOKYO lists approximately 20 minutes from Tokyo Station and 35 minutes from Shinjuku Station, with transfers; confirm current service.

  • Rail: Ginza Line, Toei Asakusa Line and Tobu Skytree Line all serve Asakusa Station. (A guide to Asakusa)
