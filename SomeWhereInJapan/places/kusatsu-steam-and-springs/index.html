A PLACE-LED FIELD GUIDE

Kusatsu, steam and springs

Approximate area reference · attraction

Watch hot spring water flow through the town center before slipping into an outdoor bath.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

Kusatsu announces itself with steam. At Yubatake, hot water pours through wooden cooling channels before flowing to town baths; mineral scent and rising vapour are part of daily life, not a staged effect. Look at the channels and the yunohana crystals that gather as water cools. A short walk leads to Netsunoyu, where yumomi performances demonstrate how bathers once cooled hot water with rhythmic paddles and song. Continue uphill to Sainokawara Park, where warm water threads through volcanic ground before collecting in an outdoor bath. The walk is compact but sloping, and winter ice changes the pace. Kusatsu is best as an overnight stay: evening steam, quiet lanes and a morning bath feel different from a quick bus stop. Mountain weather can be cooler than Tokyo; check road conditions and bathing schedules. Yubatake hot-water field. See mineral-rich water cooling through channels at the town center, especially atmospheric after dusk. Netsunoyu. Watch the local yumomi performance when scheduled and learn how the town manages hot spring water. Sainokawara Park. Follow the stream through a steaming volcanic basin to the outdoor bath; stay on signed paths.

GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

From Tokyo, travel by rail to Naganohara-Kusatsuguchi and use the JR bus to Kusatsu Onsen, or take a highway coach. The nearest airports are in Tokyo with onward rail and bus. By car, use designated town parking and check winter road advisories. Yubatake, Netsunoyu and Sainokawara Park connect on foot. Nearest rail access: Naganohara-Kusatsuguchi Station.

Start at Yubatake, see a scheduled yumomi performance at Netsunoyu, then walk uphill through Sainokawara Park. Allow 3–5 hours including bathing and the return slope.

WHERE TO STAY

Stay in the central Yubatake district to reach baths and restaurants on foot, or choose a hillside ryokan with a shuttle.

FEES & VISIT TIMEYubatake and the park walk are free; performances, public baths, private baths and parking have separate charges. Confirm current admission fees and opening hours with each venue.
