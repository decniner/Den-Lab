A PLACE-LED FIELD GUIDE

Kamakura, temple to tide

Approximate area reference · attraction

Move from temple gardens and the Great Buddha to a breezy path down to the sea.

See the place map ↓Plan your arrival

Kamakura, temple to tide is a day shaped by changing scale: the broad approach to Tsurugaoka Hachimangu Shrine gives way to lanes of small shops, then the Enoden carries you toward the coast and the open-air presence of the Great Buddha of Kamakura. At Kotoku-in, the bronze Daibutsu has stood outdoors since the hall that once covered it was destroyed; the surviving foundation stones help make the statue's setting feel less like a monument and more like a place with a long memory. A short walk away, Hasedera Temple turns the visit upward through gardens to a lookout over the town and the sea. Give the central shrine precinct the quiet of the morning, then use the railway rather than a long road transfer to reach Hase. Hydrangeas make June especially popular, while Hasedera's outlook and temple grounds change with the season. For this central-to-western route, allow about 5 to 7 hours as a planning estimate, including rail transfers, breaks and time inside the sites.

GETTING THERE

Choose the right arrival

Train: JR Yokosuka Line from Tokyo Station to Kamakura Station takes about 60 minutes; the official guide also lists about 50 minutes from Shinagawa and about 25 minutes from Yokohama. From Haneda, take Keikyu Line to Yokohama, then JR Yokosuka Line to Kamakura; from Narita, use Narita Express to Ofuna and transfer to JR Yokosuka Line. Local movement uses JR, Enoden, buses and taxis. Bus access serves the Great Buddha and Hasedera; Kamakura recommends public transport because roads near major sights are congested. By car, use public parking and expect congestion; the sites have their own restrictions. Kamakura has no airport; continue from Haneda or Narita by rail.

WHERE TO STAY

Kamakura Station and Komachi — Best for an early start at the central shrine and quick access to JR, Enoden, buses and the visitor information center.

Hase and Yuigahama — A calmer coastal base near the Great Buddha, Hasedera and Enoden Hase Station; allow for a return rail journey to central Kamakura.

FEES & VISIT TIMEThe official guide lists adult admission at ¥300 and entry inside the statue at an additional ¥50; verify current fees and hours before visiting.
