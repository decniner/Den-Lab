Borrowing against shares can fund spending without selling them. But a loan is still debt.

Imagine twenty million dollars in shares, securing a ten million dollar loan.

At a hypothetical five percent simple annual interest rate, one year costs five hundred thousand dollars. No fees or repayments.

Higher stock values do not erase the debt. Falling collateral can trigger a maintenance call and forced selling.

Under United States rules, inherited property generally receives a basis linked to its value at death. Exceptions matter.

Buy, borrow, die is the nickname. The debt survives. This animation demonstrates the mechanics, not a strategy to copy.
