# Financial explanations

Use this reference only for financial topics. Keep examples globally applicable: illustrative currency units or clearly labeled currency, no assumed country-specific accounts or rules. Use diversified, low-cost, long-term investing principles when appropriate to the message; do not add stock picks, speculation, leverage, guaranteed wealth or universal financial-independence timelines.

## Mathematics

- Calculate every displayed amount, counter endpoint, contribution total and chart point with code.
- State initial balance, contribution amount/frequency/timing, horizon, compounding convention, return assumption, and treatment of fees, taxes and inflation.
- For annual effective return `a`, the monthly equivalent is `(1+a)**(1/12)-1`. A nominal annual rate compounded monthly uses `a/12`. They are different conventions; label them and do not mix them.
- For month-end deposits `c`, update `balance = balance*(1+r)+c`. Independently verify with a closed-form annuity or power sum. For comparisons, use the same ending horizon and return convention unless the explicitly taught difference requires otherwise.
- Distinguish contributions from hypothetical growth. Verify both the precise results and the displayed rounding, including any spoken approximations.
- Label investment assumptions “Hypothetical illustration—not a forecast.” Smooth growth is a mathematical model, not actual market behavior. Losses remain possible; diversification cannot guarantee safety.

Example used in the working project: zero initial balance, 100 units at each month-end for 240 months, hypothetical 5% annual effective return converted to monthly, excluding fees/taxes/inflation. Ending balance 40,580.44853178032; contributed 24,000; hypothetical growth 16,580.44853178032. Display 40,580 and 16,580; say “about 41,000.” This is not a historical-return claim and should not automatically be reused for every finance video.

Simplified debt example: 1,000 owed at 20% nominal annual interest / 12, no fees, payments or new charges. First-month interest 16.6666666667, displayed 16.67; ending balance 1,016.67. Actual lender rules may use daily balances; do not imply the illustration quotes a particular credit product. Do not claim all debt is destructive.

## Claims and presentation

Verify material facts against primary authoritative sources; save exact supporting URLs and access dates. Historical statistics require precise periods and definitions. Do not fabricate quotations, outcomes or testimonials. Clearly distinguish editorial judgments from measured claims.

Explain that returns fluctuate, inflation reduces purchasing power, and fees/taxes matter. Income, living costs, health, family obligations, economic conditions and starting circumstances affect feasible actions and timelines. Do not shame poverty or suggest investing overrides every other financial need. Keep material assumptions readable while figures are shown, and include a legible “Educational content, not personalized financial advice” note.

For a direct money-management message, useful visual reasoning is income versus retained margin, destructive debt costs, emergency resilience, productive earning capacity, contribution versus growth, and lifestyle inflation. A closing progression such as CONTROL → MARGIN → INVEST → COMPOUND → FREEDOM must remain a process illustration, not a promise that the outcome or timeline is universal.
